What does the position mean?

The encoder measures a short window of sound. A fixed projection places it on this grid, using Mandarin recordings to learn which differences matter. The layout is learned from labeled sounds; its left/right and up/down directions have no anatomical meaning. Moving closer to a region means becoming more similar in these two dimensions. The map does not measure tongue position or grade pronunciation.

One shared map contains the vowels a / e / i / o / u / ü and the sustained consonants s / sh / x. Tone, stop consonants, and the special “i” in words such as shi are outside this version. Some regions can overlap; two dimensions cannot preserve every sound difference.

References: THCHS-30 (Apache 2.0), via changelinglab’s audio package and anyspeech’s phone timings. The timing labels have not been independently verified. Reference words retain their natural surrounding sounds.
