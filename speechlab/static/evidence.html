THE COMPLETED CORPUS STUDY

What can these measurements
actually tell us?

We tested whether measurements of a short sound distinguish two English sound categories across different speakers. This gives us a usable comparison axis. It does not yet tell us which mouth movement to make, whether a Mandarin pronunciation is correct, or whether the feedback helps someone learn.

The test

10,676 real speech segments from 80 LibriSpeech speakers. Thirty speakers fit the first models, ten different speakers selected the representation for each contrast, and forty separate speakers provided the final test. Each input was an isolated, timestamped sound, without neighboring words. Selection was frozen before the final test. The encoders may have encountered LibriSpeech during pretraining; the held-out split applies to our fitted classifiers.

We assumed the English was pronounced correctly. Word and sound boundaries were generated automatically with Montreal Forced Aligner. We filtered invalid regions and tested ±20 ms shifts; the timings have not been manually certified.

How to read the feedback

The marker is a learned S-to-SH, R-to-L, or vowel-contrast position. A marker on the S side means “more like the S examples along this contrast.” It is not a percent-correct score. An unfamiliar third sound can still land on either side.

Microphone mode finds a sustained sound by its volume, then analyzes seven 160 ms slices. The dots show those slices and the marker shows their median. Very quiet, clipped, short, or inconsistent recordings get a retry message. Simple voicing checks also flag some unsuitable inputs; they are not a general detector of incorrect pronunciation.

The corpus test used sounds from natural words. Central-crop robustness supports shorter analysis windows, but sustained microphone practice, new microphones, and arbitrary learner errors remain a separate domain. The built-in “See its result” buttons analyze the exact source sound. No recording, labeling, or training is required to inspect those results.

What each experiment measured

Approach Input → measurement
Formants Voiced sound → median F1, F2, F3 in Hz
Spectrum Sound → frequency center, spread, skew, flatness, band energies, zero crossings
Combined acoustics All of those + pitch, voicing, duration, level
Six frozen encoders Normalized sound → four ordered means of internal audio features
SPARC WavLM features → estimated articulation coordinates, pooled over time
Learned direction Each representation + known categories → a fitted binary classifier
Pairwise similarity Cosine distances → nearest category center and cross-speaker triplets
Personal references Two known-correct anchors per sound → same-speaker vs other-speaker centroids

Paid cloud assessors were not run: no credentials were supplied. We did not test language-model verbal judgments, intelligibility, anatomical accuracy, or clinical outcomes. The Qwen tests used the audio encoders alone.
